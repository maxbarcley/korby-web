The 7 Best Couple Bucket List Apps in 2026, Compared

Updated October 2026 · 8 min read

A couple bucket list app has one job a notes app does badly: hold a list that two people both add to, and help one of those ideas actually happen. The apps below all try to do that. They differ in how your partner joins, what happens after you check something off, and how they charge. We make Korby, so it’s first on the list; how we compared everything is at the end.

Quick picks

  • Turning ideas into dated plans, then memories: Korby
  • A photo-first memory book with a map: brochi
  • Streaks, reminders, and iPad support: TwoDo
  • Something playful, in many languages: Withly
  • A ready-made list of 100 dates: 100List
  • Completely free, no subscription: LoveKeeper
  • Zero setup, already on your phone: a shared list in Apple Reminders

1. Korby

Korby is built around a single path: an idea goes from Someday to Planned once you give it a date, then into a shared timeline of memories once you’ve done it. A planned idea gets a countdown on both phones and a shared checklist, so “we should go to the coast” turns into book the room, pack the jacket, leave Friday. There are 100+ ideas to start from, a separate Dreams list for the once-in-a-lifetime ones, and a way to hide a surprise plan from your partner until you reveal it.

Good for: couples who already have plenty of ideas and want fewer of them to stay ideas. Worth knowing: iPhone only, so both of you need one. It’s paid after the 14-day trial, but one subscription covers both partners. No ads, no feed, no streaks.

2. brochi

brochi is a shared list with a strong focus on what happens afterwards. One partner creates the list and invites the other with a code; plans sync in real time, and each can carry photos and notes. Its distinctive features are a dual-camera shot that captures both of you at once and a map that pins completed plans where they happened.

Good for: couples who care most about the photos and the story. Worth knowing: iPhone, in English and Spanish. The paid tier is Brochi Pro, and the listing shows weekly, monthly and annual options.

3. TwoDo

TwoDo pairs partners with an invite code and syncs the list in real time. It starts you off with 100+ ideas, lets you attach photos and notes, and keeps a visual timeline of what you’ve done. Unlike most of this list, it leans into motivation mechanics: milestone streaks and reminders.

Good for: couples who like a nudge, and anyone who wants it on iPad too. Worth knowing: English only. Streaks are motivating for some couples and a source of guilt for others, so be honest about which you are.

4. Withly

Withly is the most playful option. You build a list with tags, write a short review together after finishing an item, and raise a small virtual pet that grows as you add plans and memories. A D-Day countdown for special dates sits on the home screen.

Good for: couples who want something cute and light, and couples who don’t share English as a first language: it’s available in nine languages. Worth knowing: the listing describes a shared list but doesn’t spell out how partners connect, and it notes that the app contains loot boxes.

5. 100List

100List gives you a curated list of 100 dates to work through, from small outings to unusual ones, and lets you record a memory and a feeling for each. You can set an anniversary and send your partner a “knock” notification.

Good for: couples who would rather follow a list than build one. Worth knowing: available in English and Korean. Paid upgrades are listed as “tickets” ($3.99 to $5.99), and the listing doesn’t say whether they’re one-time. One of them is a “Connect version ticket,” so linking with your partner may be a paid step; the listing doesn’t explain how two accounts share a list.

6. LoveKeeper

LoveKeeper is mainly an anniversary and important-date tracker with multiple reminders per date, plus space for couple goals, date ideas and a bucket list. It is entirely free, with no ads and no in-app purchases, and it keeps your photos on your device rather than uploading them.

Good for: anyone who wants a free option and remembering dates matters as much as planning new ones. Worth knowing: because data stays on the device, the listing doesn’t make clear whether two phones share one list. It may work best if one of you keeps it.

7. A shared list in Apple Reminders

If you both use iPhone, you already have a free shared list. Create a list in Reminders, share it with your partner through iCloud, and either of you can add or tick items. There’s no countdown or memory timeline, but it costs nothing and takes a minute.

Good for: testing whether you’ll actually use a shared list before paying for an app.

How to choose

  • Check both phones first. Every app here is listed on the App Store, and Korby is iPhone-only. A mixed iPhone and Android couple should check Google Play before committing.
  • Find out who pays. Some apps cover both partners with one subscription; with others, it’s worth checking whether the second person needs their own plan.
  • Decide how you feel about game mechanics. Streaks, pets and scores help some couples and annoy others. Korby and LoveKeeper skip them; TwoDo and Withly lean in.
  • Look at what happens after “done.” If the point is having something to look back on, favor apps with a memory timeline or photo record.
  • Pick the one you’ll open. The best list is the one with a date on at least one item.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best couple bucket list app?

It depends on what you want from it. Korby fits couples who want to turn ideas into dated plans and keep them as memories; brochi fits couples focused on photos; TwoDo fits couples who like streaks and reminders; LoveKeeper fits anyone who wants a free option.

Is there a free couple bucket list app?

LoveKeeper is free with no in-app purchases, and Withly is free to download. A shared list in Apple Reminders is also free if you both use iPhone. Most other apps offer a free download with a paid upgrade.

Do both partners need to pay?

Not always. In Korby, one subscription covers both partners and the second person joins with a code. For other apps, check the listing or the developer’s site before you subscribe.

Can we use a couple bucket list app on iPhone and Android?

Korby is iPhone-only, so both partners need an iPhone. For a mixed couple, check each app’s Google Play listing, or use a cross-platform shared list such as a shared Google Keep note.

How many items should a couples bucket list have?

Enough to choose from, but the number matters less than dating one of them. Start with ten to twenty ideas and let the list grow, then put a date on the one you could realistically do in the next month.
