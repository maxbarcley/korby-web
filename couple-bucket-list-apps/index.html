Why most couple bucket lists never get done

A list in a notes app fails in three predictable places. Each one has research behind it, and each one is something a good app should fix.

  • No date, no plan. A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests found that deciding when and where you’ll do something has a medium-to-large effect on whether it happens, compared with only intending to (Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006). An idea without a date is a wish.
  • The waiting is half the fun, if you can see it. In an experience-sampling study of 2,266 adults, people looking forward to an experience reported more happiness, pleasantness and excitement than people looking forward to a purchase (Kumar, Killingsworth and Gilovich, 2014). A countdown you both see keeps that anticipation going.
  • New beats nice. Couples who did something novel and exciting together reported higher relationship quality afterwards than couples who did something mundane (Aron et al., 2000). The Date Night report makes the same point: skip the default dinner and a movie.

So the questions to ask of any app are simple: can both of you add ideas, can one of them get a date and a countdown, is there room for the prep, and does anything remain once you’ve done it?

2. Cupla: best when your real problem is two busy calendars

Cupla is a shared calendar first. It puts both of your schedules side by side and syncs with the calendars you already use. Its Date Planner lets you tag any event as a date, set a goal for how often you go out and get a nudge when it’s been a while, and each date becomes a Date Memory you can add a photo and notes to. Around that sit shared to-dos, wishlists for dates, gifts and trips, countdowns to key dates and an AI assistant you can switch off. If you can never find an evening you’re both free, it solves that better than anything here.

Where it shines

  • Both schedules side by side, synced with Apple, Google and Outlook calendars
  • A Date Planner with a date goal and gentle nudges; past dates become Date Memories with photos
  • Wishlists for dates, gifts and trips, including ideas saved from Instagram or TikTok
  • Countdowns to key dates and upcoming trips
  • Runs on iPhone and Android; one Premium subscription per couple

Where it falls short

  • The bucket list is one feature among many rather than the center of the app
  • Wishlist ideas and prep to-dos sit apart from the dated plan
  • The free plan only shows a limited window of your partner’s items around today

3. Lovewick: best for conversation questions and date ideas

Lovewick is built around question cards and a big library of date ideas. You can browse 750+ free ideas, filter them by time, cost or long distance, save them to shared wishlists, put dates on the ones you schedule and hide surprises from your partner. Checked-off ideas feed a memory timeline.

Where it shines

  • 750+ free date ideas with useful filters, especially for long distance
  • 1,000+ free conversation questions
  • Shared wishlists with dates, surprises and a memory timeline
  • Runs on iPhone and Android; the developer says one Plus subscription covers both partners

Where it falls short

  • Questions come first; planning is a secondary feature
  • No per-idea prep checklist mentioned in the listing
  • Some long-time users were unhappy when free daily questions were cut back
  • English only

4. Apple Reminders: best free shared to-do list

If you both use iPhone, share a list in Reminders through iCloud and you can both add, edit and tick off ideas. Due dates and subtasks cover the basics of planning. It won’t suggest ideas or count down to anything, and completed items keep no photos or notes about how it went.

Where it shines

  • Works on iPhone, iPad and Mac

Where it falls short

Frequently asked questions

What is the best couple bucket list app?

For actually doing what’s on your list, Korby: it’s the only option here that keeps the idea, a dated countdown, a shared prep checklist and the memory with photos on one plan. One subscription covers both of you. Cupla is better if your main problem is coordinating calendars and keeping a date-night habit, and Lovewick if you mainly want conversation questions and date ideas.

Is there a free couple bucket list app?

Apple Notes, Apple Reminders, Google Keep and Pinterest are free ways to share a list. Cupla and Lovewick have free plans with paid upgrades. Korby is free for 14 days, then paid.

Do both partners have to pay?

No, not for the three paid apps here. Korby covers both of you with one subscription and your partner joins with a code. Cupla’s help center and Lovewick’s developer both say the same for their subscriptions.

Can we use a couple bucket list app on iPhone and Android?

Cupla, Lovewick, Google Keep and Pinterest work on both. Korby is iPhone-only for now, so a mixed couple is better served by one of those.

Why use an app instead of a shared note?

A note holds ideas but doesn’t help them happen. Research on planning finds that deciding when and where you’ll do something makes it meaningfully more likely to happen, and looking forward to an experience is part of its enjoyment. An app built for this gives each idea a date, a countdown and a checklist, and keeps the memory afterwards.
