The Best Apps for Couples in 2026, Sorted by What You Need

Updated October 2026 · 8 min read

Many “best apps for couples” lists rank twenty apps against each other as if they did the same thing. They don’t. A shared calendar and a counseling app solve completely different problems, and installing five of them mostly gives you five more notifications.

So this list is sorted by the problem. Find the one that sounds like you, try one app, and ignore the rest. (Korby, under “doing more together,” is ours; our method is at the end.)

For planning your weeks: Cupla

Cupla puts both partners’ schedules side by side and syncs with Apple, Google and Outlook calendars, so you can see when you’re actually both free without asking. Around that it adds shared to-dos and lists, reminders you can assign to each other, wishlists for date ideas and gifts, countdowns to key dates, and reminders to protect time together. It also includes an AI assistant, which you can switch off.

Choose it if your main problem is that you rarely know when the other person is free.

For doing more together: Korby

Korby is a shared bucket list for two. Both of you add the trips, restaurants and small plans you keep talking about; when you give one a date, it gets a countdown on both phones and a shared checklist; when you’ve done it, it becomes a dated memory with photos on a shared timeline. One subscription covers both partners, there are no ads or feed, and it’s iPhone-only.

Choose it if you have no shortage of ideas but very few of them ever get a date. We compare it with similar apps in our guide to couple bucket list apps.

For talking about more than logistics: Paired

Paired sends you both a daily question. Each of you answers, and you only see each other’s answer once you’ve both replied, which makes it a reliable conversation starter. It also tracks important dates, and the Premium subscription adds guided journeys on specific topics plus games and quizzes.

Choose it if most of your conversations are about who’s picking up what, and you’d like a low-effort prompt to go further.

For working through harder things: Lasting

Lasting offers structured, guided sessions for couples on topics like communication, conflict, repair, trust and money. A free five-session Foundations series lets you try it before paying, and Premium unlocks the full library for two people.

Choose it if you keep having the same argument and want a structured way to work on it. If you’re dealing with something serious, a licensed couples therapist is the better route.

For date ideas and questions in one place: Lovewick

Lovewick combines more than 1,000 free questions with more than 750 free date ideas, which you can filter by time, cost or long-distance suitability and save to shared wishlists. It also keeps a memory timeline and notes on your partner’s preferences. Pairing syncs content between phones; answering questions works without it. Lovewick Plus is listed at $29.99 a year.

Choose it if you want inspiration first, and especially if you’re long-distance.

For shared money: Honeydue

Honeydue lets couples see balances, budgets and bills in one place while each person chooses how much of their own accounts to share. It sets spending limits by category, reminds you when bills are due, and lets you split expenses. It’s free, with optional monthly tips, and supports most US banks.

Choose it if money talks are where things get tense, and seeing the same numbers would help.

If you don’t want another app

If you both use iPhone, three free tools cover a surprising amount:

  • A shared calendar in the Calendar app for plans with dates.
  • A shared list in Reminders for groceries, chores, or a simple bucket list.
  • A shared album in Photos so both of you can add pictures from the same trip.

They don’t talk to each other, which is the main reason dedicated apps exist. But they cost nothing and are a good test of whether you’d use something more.

The case for using just one

It’s easy to install three couples apps at once, open them for a week, and then stop opening any of them. Pick the problem that bothers you most, choose the app for that, and give it a month before adding anything else.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best app for couples?

There isn’t one app that does everything well. Cupla is strongest for shared schedules, Korby for turning shared ideas into plans and memories, Paired for daily conversation, Lasting for structured relationship work, Lovewick for date ideas and questions, and Honeydue for shared finances.

Is there a free app for couples?

Honeydue is free with optional tips, Cupla, Lasting and Lovewick have free tiers or free content, and Paired is a free download with a Premium subscription. If you both use iPhone, a shared calendar, Reminders list and Photos album are free and already installed.

What is the best app for long-distance couples?

Lovewick is a good starting point because its date ideas can be filtered for long-distance, and Paired’s daily questions work the same whether you live together or apart.

Should couples use more than one app?

Usually not at first. Start with the one that addresses your biggest friction point and only add another once the first has become a habit.
